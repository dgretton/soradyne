## Contract conclusions

**Calibration need not remain an active solver variable in every representation.**
In this exact linear example, integrating it out into a joint observation covariance
gives the same answer. Its shared dependence cannot disappear: independent per-record
noise inflation is incorrect. Future calibration revisions additionally need retained
conditional/model information or authorized replay; today's collapsed target estimate
plus identities can be insufficient even when today's mean and covariance are exact.
This is a sufficient alternative in one model, not a universal finite summary for
nonlinear camera calibration or drift.

**Withdrawal must reach the support of a learned calibration.** A target result may
depend on a withdrawn sensor/time/field through its calibration, even when all its own
target measurements remain permitted. An old calibration derivative is reusable only
under the specified rights: raw deletion with retained-derivative permission (P2) and
strict contribution withdrawal (P3) intentionally give different outcomes.

**Field-level identity alone is insufficient for field-level reconstruction.** With
correlated fields, the surviving likelihood needs the proper marginal noise model.
Dropping rows from an information matrix can preserve forbidden values or invent
precision. Retaining permitted field values with the appropriate noise/dependency
model, an adequate precomputed marginal, or replay can preserve the operation.

These findings refine T01. There is **no new architectural contradiction**, no new
requirement to keep every calibration live, and no production or owning-contract edit.

### Shared calibration across two times

Let b0~N(0,tau), d~N(0,q), b1=b0+d, with independent observation errors of variance r:

```
z0 = g*x + b0     + e0
z1 = g*x + b0 + d + e1
```

The candidate forms an information system on (x,b0,d). The independent reference
integrates out b0,d and uses covariance
`V=[[tau+r,tau],[tau,tau+q+r]]` for the two measurements, then explicit 2x2 GLS.
All **32** fixed combinations (tau,q in {1,4}; r,g in {1,2}; z=(0,6) or (1,4)) agree
exactly. No floating tolerances or sampled coverage are involved.

For tau=4,q=r=g=1,z=(0,6):

| Method | Mean x | Variance x |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained joint calibration factors | 2 | 14/3 |
| Integrated calibration with joint V | 2 | 14/3 |
| Incorrect independent noise inflation | 30/11 | 30/11 |
| Incorrect omission of drift | 3 | 9/2 |

An independent full-joint check gives means (2,0,2), Var(b0)=4, Var(d)=2/3,
Cov(x,b0)=-4, Cov(x,d)=-1/3 and Cov(b0,d)=0. The difference z1-z0 constrains d;
with flat x, the shared offset b0 remains uncertain. An exact x marginal here does
not validate a representation for every later query about b0 or d.

The observation-space alternative retains both observations and their joint model;
it is raw-equivalent information, not a privacy or compression gain. For a fixed
revision, V suffices for this x query. To change tau, q or g later, the retained
state must also supply the model's response/covariance recipe or a lawful rebuild.

### Replacing calibration information is not relabeling or adding evidence

With the old parameters, z=(0,6) and z=(1,4) both yield exactly x=(2,14/3).
Their retained marginal, model stamp and identity-only manifest can be identical.
Replace q=1 with q=4, with no new measurements:

| World | Required new mean x | Required new variance x |
|---|---:|---:|
| z=(0,6) | 1 | 29/6 |
| z=(1,4) | 3/2 | 29/6 |

No function of that identical collapsed schema can choose both answers. Retained
pre-elimination factors or the observation-space recipe do distinguish them.
Identity-only means exactly that: the witness does not include hidden raw values,
factor coefficients or a reconstructible archive in the insufficient schema.

Incorrectly appending the replacement drift prior to the old prior combines them
into effective variance 4/5 and gives x=(15/7,65/14) in the first world. A replacement
must identify what it supersedes; it is not another independent observation merely
because its version is newer.

An alternative revision changes gain g from 1 to 2. Authorized reconstruction gives
x=(1,7/6); stamping old coefficients with the new revision leaves x=(2,14/3).
A different hardware/calibration identity is rejected by this fixture unless the
caller supplies an explicit binding. The policy choosing which historical records
a new calibration applies to is an input, not inferred from a timestamp or number.

### Sensor, acquisition-time and field selectors with learned calibration

Base b~N(0,4). Calibration training c=6, variance 1, comes from sensor A at time 10,
field `calibration-offset`. It yields b=(24/5,4/5). Target observations y=x+b+noise,
variance 1 each, are (0,A,9), (6,B,10), (12,A,11), all field `signal`.
Times are exact ticks on one clock; the production clock convention remains a contract
obligation. Acquisition time does not become derivative creation or publication time.

The candidate rebuilds a two-variable information matrix from surviving records;
the independent oracle conditions b on surviving training then computes
mean(targets)-mean(b), Var(b)+1/n. All four selectors match exactly:

| P3 selection | Surviving target records | Mean x | Variance x |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| None, initial answer | A9, B10, A11 | 6/5 | 17/15 |
| Sensor A | B10 | 6 | 5 |
| Time range [10,11) | A9, A11 | 6 | 9/2 |
| Field calibration-offset | A9, B10, A11 | 6 | 13/3 |
| Sensor A AND [10,11) AND calibration-offset | A9, B10, A11 | 6 | 13/3 |

Every removal includes c, so the shared calibration influence is rebuilt from the
remaining permitted base prior. The range includes time 10 and excludes 11. The field
case preserves all target measurements but changes their inference through calibration.
Changing c's value before the experiment changes the initial answer and leaves the
proper post-withdrawal answer unchanged. This checks dependence on the actual value,
not just whether the displayed lineage omits an ID.

Keeping the old learned calibration under P3 incorrectly preserves x=(6/5,17/15).
The guarded derivative path returns unavailable before using its numerical payload;
permitted target records permit rebuilding x=(6,13/3). With no surviving target
numerical state supplied, that path remains unavailable. It does not manufacture
values from the IDs or solve the broader inverse-recovery problem.

Under a separate **P2** fixture, an explicit right to this derivative survives removal
of raw c access, and x=(6/5,17/15) remains usable. That is not P3 withdrawal. The grant
is a trusted fixture input, not something inferred from the object's label, covariance
or mathematical cancellation. Future policy must decide the treatment of calibration
descendants. A sufficient derivative can still expose raw-equivalent information.

### Correlated fields

Fields u=0,v=4 both observe x with known, independently authorized covariance
`R=[[1,1/2],[1/2,1]]`. Full x=(2,3/4). After v is withdrawn, the correct retained
likelihood uses u and its **marginal** variance R_uu=1, giving x=(0,1).

Slicing the observation-space precision and full information vector instead gives
x=(-2,3/4): v still affects the answer. Rebuilding the information vector using u
but keeping the sliced precision gives x=(0,3/4): no v value is used, yet uncertainty
is too small. Changing v to -100 leaves the correct answer unchanged and changes
the first incorrect answer. Correct processing does not read v after withdrawal.

If R itself were estimated from withdrawn data, R would require its own dependency
and permission treatment; this witness assumes otherwise. A field-splittable schema
must retain sufficient numerical likelihood information, not just split identifiers.

## Minimal choices to carry into synthesis

| Obligation | Smallest supported direction / alternatives |
|---|---|
| Evidence selection | Stable record/revision plus source sensor, semantic field/component and original acquisition time/clock. Declare interval boundaries and supported selection granularity. |
| Shared calibration | Logical calibration identity distinct from a belief/model revision; record which observations share it, temporal segments/drift relationships and model applicability. A joint factor or adequate correlated likelihood may replace an active variable. |
| Calibration revision | Bind artifacts to the response/noise model and superseded contribution; retain rebuildable factors, an adequate model-dependent statistic or authorized replay. A stamp alone cannot update numerical state. |
| Learned calibration | Retain dependency support through calibration to downstream results. A changed support set requires a new derived result identity/revision, independent of the physical calibration identity. |
| Fine withdrawal | Preserve numerical state at the promised sensor/time/field partition, or a representation/replay path that can reconstruct its surviving likelihood. Covariance coupling must be handled, not silently discarded. |
| Permission and absence | Distinguish raw deletion, retained-derivative use and contribution withdrawal; expose unavailable or explicitly reduced coverage when required lawful state is absent. |

This does not require an ID for every image byte or indefinite raw retention. Choose
the selection units the interface promises, and declare larger units when fine removal
is unsupported. If arbitrary time cuts are promised after raw expiry, fixed unsplittable
time buckets alone are inadequate unless another sufficient retained/replay path exists
(0009). Numerical availability and lawful retention are separate requirements.

For eventual proposals: nestbox-ng GLOSSARY's Calibration/Operation/Provenance and
CONVENTIONS §§4–6 need explicit dependency, approximation and revision meaning alongside
the existing calibration-variable and covariance language. SUBSTRATE custody/revocation
must expose enough generic retention/acceptance semantics for those application choices.
Shared-flow §§1–3,5 already separate opaque identity, rights, retention and mathematical
acceptance; any additions should preserve generic versioned dependencies and atomic
acceptance, not teach the provider camera calibration or covariance algebra. Proposals
will be consolidated in SYNTHESIS.md; neither owning contract was edited.
