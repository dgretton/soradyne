## Findings for the data model and contracts

**A result must bind one compatible set of dependencies and uncertainty.** Matching
coordinate epochs, selecting each latest edge, or comparing local revision numbers
does not establish that compatibility. A parent/child dependency can require a specific
other revision; covariance must describe those same contributions. The compatible set
may include an unchanged independent edge. This does not require one global revision
counter, simultaneous computation, or a global transaction.

**An opaque result identity can bind private details without exposing them.** The
binding needs the query, numerical result, representation/model, actual inputs,
calibration/dependency revisions, coordinate epochs, joint uncertainty and acceptance
decision. The reader may receive a selected public view. Equal means and covariances
do not make two manifests interchangeable. A previously issued identity is also not
perpetual authority to reuse its contents; current permission is a separate check.

**Coordinate epochs and physical-state epochs need distinguishable meanings.** A
known change of coordinates can preserve physical geometry and motion while changing
all the numbers. A physical bump changes physical state. An unknown or ambiguous reset
cannot supply a known relation merely by receiving a new epoch ID. Retaining old/new
identities, effective boundary, event kind/knowledge, relation direction and uncertainty
leaves later consumers able to respond correctly. No jump-only rule can establish cause.

These are refinements of the existing representation and validity contracts, not a new
architectural contradiction. Cause detection, distributed snapshot construction and
physical consumer response remain deferred.

## I08: nested revisions with an independent exact oracle

Three scalar translation edges map B into C, C into P, and P into W:

```
outer (W <- P) = 10 + k
middle (P <- C) = -3 - k + e
leaf (C <- B) = 2 + f
```

k,e,f are independent in each coherent world. e has mean 0, variance 1; f mean 0,
variance 1/4. Old k has mean 0, variance 4; revised k mean 100, variance 9. By direct
substitution the W-relative B coordinate is **9+e+f**, mean **9 m**, variance **5/4 m²**
under either calibration. The candidate instead adds edge means and their supplied
joint covariance. It does not use the original-variable oracle to repair results.

The outer/middle covariance is -4 or -9, respectively. Leaf covariance with either
is zero. All frames retain the same coordinate epochs. Outer versions are 1/2,
middle versions 11/12, and the unchanged leaf version is 7. Each middle explicitly
names its outer and shared-calibration revision. A joint-uncertainty artifact binds
the ordered edge-revision tuple and scalar representation; its diagonal must agree
with the individual edge variances. This is trusted numerical payload, not truth
established by a name or manifest.

These are already-solved edge estimates with a declared joint distribution. The
witness composes a query; it does not inject a parent's posterior as independent
evidence, define a nested estimator's message schedule, or authorize republishing
another node's solution. Those provenance/feedback constraints from earlier cycles
and the owning contracts still apply.

All eight combinations were evaluated. Only the two compatible combinations are
accepted. The intentionally unbound composer gives:

| Outer / middle / covariance world | Mean | Variance | Checked result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Old / old / old | 9 | 5/4 | Accepted |
| Old / old / new | 9 | -35/4 | Rejected |
| Old / new / old | -91 | 25/4 | Rejected |
| Old / new / new | -91 | -15/4 | Rejected |
| New / old / old | 109 | 25/4 | Rejected |
| New / old / new | 109 | -15/4 | Rejected |
| New / new / old | 9 | 45/4 | Rejected |
| New / new / new | 9 | 5/4 | Accepted |

A negative variance is an obviously invalid combination; the positive-but-wrong
variances and +/-100 m false corrections matter too. Neither matching endpoint epochs
nor a unchanged mean catches all failures. No motion, noise samples or controller
response is simulated by these revision changes.

All six arrival orders of new outer, middle and covariance were tested, with three
prefixes each. The explicitly requested new revision is unavailable at the first two
prefixes and accepted only at the third: **12 unavailable, 6 accepted**. Independently
requesting the retained old revision is allowed at all 18 prefixes under the stipulated
continuing permission. It is labeled old and cannot fulfill an explicit new-revision
request. An announced target revision tuple is assumed; discovery of the desired
snapshot is not implemented here.

The example therefore requires a dependency-closed, compatible selection and complete
acceptance boundary, not a total ordering of all domain activity. Independent leaf 7
can be reused in either selection. Exactly which historical/as-of result a caller may
accept remains a query policy; a provider must not silently substitute it for current
or specifically requested state. Construction/publication atomicity is next cycle F04.

### Authority and public result views

The fixture checks current permitted input IDs before composition. A second policy
that withdraws old calibration support rejects the old coherent result and allows
the new one. Another policy revision with unchanged rights still allows the old
result. This separates historical compatibility from current entitlement without
requiring every harmless policy revision to erase all caches.

A bounded in-memory issuer binds immutable complete answers to symbolic opaque IDs.
Old and new answers have the same numerical result but different IDs; exact reissue
of the same answer is idempotent. The public projection contains result ID, query
endpoints/epochs and time, units/representation, mean/variance and issue-time validity.
It omits private input IDs, calibration and intermediate edge dependencies, and policy
identifiers. The issuer's private binding retains those details.

This is a schema/binding witness, **not cryptographic privacy or grant enforcement**.
The fixture tokens are predictable local labels, not security capabilities. An output
reader's entitlement is assumed; no claim is made about side-channel leakage, inferable
input values, signatures, authenticated manifests, current-authority discovery or
authorization for provenance inspection. Neither an opaque ID nor a passed metadata
check validates the mathematical payload. Input-model/representation semantics here
are fixed by the declared scalar type; arbitrary model changes need their own bindings.

## S02: a reset is not a physical observation

For an exact known planar change of coordinates,
`p_new=R*p_old+t`, R is a 90-degree rotation and t=(100,-20) m. The scene changes as:

| Quantity | Old coordinates | New coordinates |
|---|---|---|
| Point | (1,2) | (98,-19) |
| Target | (4,6) | (94,-16) |
| Obstacle | (10,3) | (97,-10) |
| Physical velocity | (2,-1) | (1,2) |
| Point covariance | diag(1,4) | diag(4,1) |

Target distance squared stays **25 m²**; obstacle distance squared stays **82 m²**.
Moving only the point into the new coordinates while retaining the old target gives
9461 m². Velocity rotates as a vector, covariance as R*C*Rᵀ; a separate off-diagonal
test checks the sign change. No actual physical time elapses at the coordinate switch.
Differencing the two point representations over one publication tick invents (97,-21)
metres per publication tick, which is not the physical velocity or a new observation.

An even smaller ambiguity witness has the same displayed point jump **1 -> 101**:

| Event | Target afterward | Target minus point afterward |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pure origin change of +100 | 110 | 9 |
| Physical bump with target fixed | 10 | -91 |

The point trace alone cannot distinguish those worlds. Event kind must come from
additional justified information, not a preferred smoothing rule. Treating a real bump
as a chart change would hide changed geometry; treating the chart change as a bump
would invent it. This is an information/semantics witness, not a bump detector.

The four event records distinguish known re-expression, physical bump, unknown reset
and ambiguous cause. A re-expression changes coordinate epoch while preserving the
motion epoch and supplies a directed relation. A bump changes the physical-state
epoch while the coordinate chart may remain unchanged; an absent reset bridge there
means no chart change is needed, **not** that old physical state may be reused. Unknown
or ambiguous cases declare the relation/physical continuity unknown. No cross-epoch
continuation is fabricated; independently supported within-new-epoch relative queries
may remain usable. Event intervals are [0,10) and [10,20); time 10 belongs to the new one.

An uncertain bridge also introduces shared dependence. In a separate exact scalar
case, a common translation error of variance 9 adds to point/target variances 1 and 4.
The new absolute variances are 10 and 13, cross covariance 9, and relative variance
remains **5**. Treating the bridge as independent copies gives **23**. Thus relation
identity and uncertainty/correlation need to travel together. This assumes the bridge
error is independent of the original point/target errors; a relation inferred from
those same observations would require the appropriate additional cross terms.

## Minimal obligations for synthesis

1. **Separate identities:** physical/frame identity, coordinate realization/epoch,
   physical-state epoch, evidence/model/calibration revision, and result revision must
   be distinguishable. They may be packed into structured IDs; separate global counters
   or separate wire objects are not established as necessary by this witness.
2. **Bind the complete answer:** exact contributing revisions and dependency constraints,
   input/model/representation identity, uncertainty/correlation, query direction/time
   and validity, and the acceptance decision belong to one immutable result binding.
   Intermediate details may be private; an opaque public revision must still resolve
   unambiguously for the authorized issuer/auditor. Identity-only metadata is not
   sufficient numerical state for recomputation (earlier T01 witnesses still apply).
3. **State compatibility and fallback:** accept only a compatible snapshot within its
   applicability interval. A caller may explicitly accept an older permitted snapshot;
   do not call it the requested newer result. Current-use permission is distinct from
   the authority recorded when an artifact was built.
4. **Describe discontinuities:** give event kind and knowledge/ambiguity, affected
   scope, effective interval, old/new epochs, and directed relation with uncertainty
   when known. Mark absent relations as unknown or not applicable, not identity by
   default. Result/state references need to bind the relevant event/epochs consistently.
5. **Preserve geometry on known re-expression:** state, targets, obstacles, vectors and
   uncertainty use the same relation, including its shared error. A physical bump is
   a fresh-state problem; unknown cause does not authorize smoothing away the change.

Nestbox's existing CONVENTIONS covariance and half-open time rules and GLOSSARY lookup
chain provide useful foundations. Proposed refinements belong to its query/epoch and
nesting contracts. Shared-flow §§1–3,5 can preserve opaque revisions, dependencies,
representation and acceptance metadata without understanding transforms. Formal owning
contract proposals remain for SYNTHESIS.md; none are edited in this cycle.
