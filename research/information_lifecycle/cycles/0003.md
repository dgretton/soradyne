## Finding

Immediate invalidation and authorized local braking do not by themselves guarantee
enough physical stopping margin. Seven of nine finite-jerk/delay variants of the 0002
necessary-correction event cross the same boundary. A separate necessary lower bound
shows that all seven crossings are unavoidable under this scalar model's assumptions,
not merely shortcomings of the chosen smooth stop. The two remaining variants fit.
Every run still makes alignment unavailable immediately and clears its numerical state
under P3. Physical continuation is measured separately from continued information use.

This extends T02. A consumer contract needs to state the operating conditions under
which its fallback is feasible, including state, response limits and margin before
information loss. These results do not select a production controller or establish
hardware safety. They also do not justify retaining forbidden estimates.

## Method and independent calculation

At invalidation, position is reset to zero in a fixed local frame. The planner sees
only independent, still-authorized local speed, measured acceleration, acceleration
limit A, jerk limit J, and physical response delay d. Clearance, alignment and global
task target are absent from its inputs. During d the actuator holds its measured
acceleration; the old target is not reevaluated. After d, a constant negative jerk
ramp, optional constant deceleration, and positive jerk ramp bring both velocity and
acceleration to zero. There is no velocity clipping, reversal or acceleration reset.

For initial nonnegative speed v and acceleration a, after delay:

```
v_d = v + a*d
x_d = v*d + a*d*d/2
b = min(A, sqrt(J*v_d + a*a/2))
t_down = (a+b)/J
t_hold = max(0, (v_d + a*a/(2*J) - b*b/J)/b)
t_up = b/J
```

The zero-motion case has no braking phases. The simulator integrates constant jerk
exactly within event-aligned steps. A separately structured distance oracle first
removes positive acceleration: its additional distance is v_d*a/J+a^3/(3J^2), and
speed then is w=v_d+a^2/(2J). The remaining acceleration profile is symmetric, so its
distance is w*T/2, with T=2*sqrt(w/J) when triangular or T=w/A+A/J when saturated.
Adding x_d gives the independently calculated candidate stopping distance. This is
elementary integration for the specified profile, not an imported controller theorem.

The necessary lower bound uses a different endpoint: apply the most negative
permitted acceleration at every time, a_min(t)=max(a-J*t,-A), until the first zero
of velocity. Every allowed trajectory has acceleration at least a_min, hence speed
and forward displacement at least those integrals up to that first zero. Therefore a
clearance smaller than this displacement must be crossed in this model. This bound
ends with negative acceleration and is not itself a feasible stopped state. Clipping
velocity and resetting acceleration at that instant violates finite jerk. A mutation
test detects that defect. The gap above the lower bound remains explicitly unresolved
by this analysis; no global optimality claim is made for the smooth candidate.

starting accelerations, three jerk limits and three delays. Four clearances give 216

and 54 upper-corner parameter runs. No random seeds, fitted parameters, statistical

The acceleration-only estimate v^2/(2A) predicts positive margin in 162 comparisons;

### Parameter bounds, accuracy and permission

The declared parameter allowance increases initial speed by up to 0.05 m/s and
physical delay by up to 0.02 s, with acceleration and limits fixed. The candidate
distance formula is monotone in speed and delay in this nonnegative-acceleration
domain: each positive-acceleration term is nondecreasing and the symmetric distance
is nondecreasing in w. Its upper corner therefore bounds the whole rectangle for
this profile, not merely four sampled corners. All four corner calculations and
upper-corner trajectories agreed. Eight nominal positive-margin comparisons lose
their margin with this allowance. The anchor with J=40,d=0.05 still fits, but its
margin drops from 0.116458 m to 0.023533 m.

This sensitivity does not model a controller planning from an erroneous velocity:
each trajectory uses its actual initial state to plan. Nor does it supply probabilistic
estimator covariance, a wall-location bound or a guarantee under changing actuator
limits. It is a conditional parameter envelope. Alignment reports no mean or bound
while unavailable. No target-tracking accuracy or task completion is claimed after
the stop; numerical accuracy here means agreement with the kinematic oracle.

Across all 216 integrated trajectories, maximum distance disagreement with the oracle
was 5.47e-14 m; time disagreement was 3.38e-14 s; terminal speed and acceleration
residuals were at most 1.85e-14 m/s and 2.94e-15 m/s^2. Acceleration and jerk limits
held within the declared tolerance. Nominal clearance classifications agreed at all
three timesteps. This checks sampling/integration, not discrete controller robustness:
phase switching is event-aligned and exact at every timestep.

The P3 consumer clears its alignment state at t=0, exposes zero available-alignment
steps afterward, and rejects a late new estimate. The planner has no alignment input.
The simulated actuator still moves during the response delay; it does not query or
blend the withdrawn reference. This is conditional on independent local sensing and
permitted residual physical actuation. It is not a claim to erase past physical effects
or previously delivered plaintext. Precision gating and withdrawal remain distinct:
the tests reproduce the 0002 gate event state and separately check P3 erasure. The
0002 withdrawal case's negative initial acceleration is outside this cycle's domain.

## Design implications, limits and next action

T02 has stronger conditional evidence, not a new architecture contradiction. The
application/consumer needs an explicit feasibility contract for lost information:
what local state remains usable, physical delay/response limits, and which operating
envelope allows a stop or other fallback. In particular, the provider cannot promise
physical safety merely by delivering an immediate validity event. Margin must exist
before an invalidation; a smooth transition cannot create missing space afterward.
The present planner does not enforce such an envelope because the wall is evaluator-
only. Preemptive constraints, independent local obstacle sensing or another permitted
fallback are hypotheses for later consumer work, not features established here.

Soradyne's part stays generic: explicit versioned permission/validity events and
declared delivery/degradation behavior. Numerical stopping, geometry and whether a
consumer can continue a task remain application responsibilities. Neither contract
is silently modified by these experiments. Network delay before learning revocation,
negative initial acceleration, local sensing that depends on withdrawn evidence,
multi-axis dynamics, stochastic errors and real actuator behavior remain untested.

Next is the previously queued small entitlement/replay ledger (R01/R02/R04/R08).
Use the same exact scalar evidence under P1, P2, P3 and overlapping scoped grants;
compare mean/uncertainty and retained/usable records against an authorized-only oracle.
Test one grant disappearing while another remains, stale cache delivery after a
re-grant, and explicit history gaps. Do not continue expanding the motion model in
the next cycle; its bounded question has a useful answer and the broader plan remains.
