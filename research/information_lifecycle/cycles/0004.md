## Findings

A small scoped entitlement ledger with exact recomputation handles the six specified
linear fixtures without advanced estimation machinery. It matches the independent
oracle in all 36 scenario/arrival-order combinations, across 570 event/query checks.
The useful result is the separation of rights and evidence identities, not a claim
that this prototype implements the full authorization system.

1. Losing one grant must not destroy evidence still held and usable through another.
   Two grants for one observation also must not create two independent measurements.
2. Raw retention, raw inference, summary retention and summary inference need separate
   decisions. A specifically authorized frozen derivative can remain exact after raw
   deletion. Retaining its bytes alone does not authorize inference with it.
3. A re-grant needs an explicit historical range and generation. Old delivery tokens
   and cached results must not silently revive the old entitlement. Legitimate replay
   under the new grant can fill a gap, without treating an observation as new evidence
   every time it is delivered.
4. Once a mixed summary is no longer usable, the answer may become unavailable until
   lawful replay supplies the surviving component. Permission to use that component
   does not reconstruct its missing value.
5. Numerical correctness is insufficient as an authorization check. The historical-
   token negative control can accept and retain forbidden data while still reporting
   exactly the authorized mean and variance.

No new architectural contradiction was established. T01 gains a concrete policy/replay
fixture; the distinction between P2 derivative retention and P3 withdrawal remains an
explicit policy choice. The stopping/transition obligations from T02 still apply.

## Model and independent oracle

One local materializer, three scopes (flow/audience/purpose), and a fixed dimensionless
scalar. Each distinct raw observation has independent unit Gaussian noise under the
declared model; there is no informative prior. The synthetic values are a1=10, a2=14,
a3=100, a4=18, b1=0. They deliberately expose changed information sets; they are not
drawn from a statistical trial and do not validate covariance calibration or outliers.

The candidate uses scalar information form: precision is the number of distinct unit-
variance observations and information is their sum. A summary stores these aggregates
and dependency identities, not the individual values. Composition selects authorized,
nonoverlapping summaries and then authorized raw observations not already represented.
Partial summary overlap is explicitly unsupported in this cycle.

The evaluator separately specifies expected retained and represented identity sets
after each policy event. Its mean is the direct arithmetic average of evaluator-only
original values, and its variance is 1/n. It never asks the candidate's authorization
helpers to decide the expected answer. Equality is exact using Fraction arithmetic;
there are no fitted thresholds or floating tolerances. Deleted values remain only in
the evaluator/source fixture, not in the ledger's numerical input store. An explicitly
authorized source replay is required to deliver them again.

The policy model deliberately chooses:

- Immutable grants and increasing per-scope policy revisions; changed/re-granted
  rights need a newer grant generation. The same observation identity survives that
  generation change. A unit test re-grants and replays the same old identity lawfully.
- A union of valid retention paths for physical storage, with exact-scope inference
  checks. The private archive cannot lend its inference permission to another purpose.
- Explicit grants for immutable summary identities. Neither the word "summary" nor
  raw-input permission implicitly authorizes arbitrary derivatives or disclosure.
- Current delivery tokens, plus resource-range checks. Rejecting an old token even
  when the same bytes could be reauthorized is this fixture's admission contract;
  it is not claimed as the only possible protocol design.
- Atomic local policy installation and cleanup before the next query. Current-result
  caches are stamped by scope, policy revision and input-store revision. The global
  store revision is conservative and can invalidate unrelated caches when bytes change.
  Explicit queries for older versions would require a separate API/policy.

The future-cutoff fixture defines its permitted history by source sequence (a1/a2),
not wall-clock measurement or receipt time. Record measurement labels 1..4 and local
event times 10+event_index are recorded separately. This tests that declared frontier;
it does not settle late/backdated-data semantics for a wall-clock cutoff. Those remain
part of the later R07 work. The issuer and its authority are trusted in this model.

## Results by scenario

Each fixture runs all six initial arrival permutations, with duplicate delivery of
each initial observation. Their later outcomes agree. The table gives canonical
results; mean/variance refer to the declared linear model, not physical accuracy.

| Event or policy | Mean | Variance | Information/retention outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Initial a1,a2,b1 | 8 | 1/3 | Each identity counted once, despite replay/grant overlap |
| P1 cutoff to a1/a2 | 8 | 1/3 | Retain existing information; reject a3 under both old and out-of-range current tokens |
| P2 delete raw A, explicitly keep a12 | 8 | 1/3 | Raw A absent; the authorized summary preserves its contribution |
| Retain a12 bytes but remove its inference right | 0 | 1 | Summary still stored, but only b1 enters the answer |
| Remove one of two alignment grants for A | 8 | 1/3 | Other grant preserves lawful use and storage |
| Remove the last alignment grant for A | 0 | 1 | Private/archive and audit still produce 12 with variance 1/2; A remains stored |
| P3 revoke mixed ab12, b1 raw survives | 0 | 1 | Rebuild immediately; reject old cached output and summary replay |
| P3 revoke mixed ab12 after all raw was deleted | unavailable | unavailable | b1 is entitled but missing; no proper estimate is invented |
| Replay b1 under its renewed grant | 0 | 1 | Recovery uses newly admitted lawful evidence |
| Re-grant a3/a4; only a4 has arrived | 9 | 1/2 | Best effort explicitly reports missing a3; required-coverage query is unavailable |
| Replay a3 under generation 2 | 118/3 | 1/3 | Required coverage restored; a1/a2 stay excluded |

Strict withdrawal changes the answer from 8 to 0 and variance from 1/3 to 1 at
the same local policy event. There is no smoothing interval during which old evidence
remains usable. A separate equal-value test removes one of two zero-valued observations:
the mean stays zero while variance changes from 1/2 to 1. A mean-change detector alone
would miss that loss of information.

Authorized replay can also produce a large revision: in the gap fixture, mean 9 changes
to 118/3, a jump of 91/3 scalar units, when a3 arrives. The event traces record these
jumps alongside permission, variance, missing coverage and availability. This is not
a physical controller simulation. It establishes neither acceptable motion nor a
license to smooth across forbidden history. The jump metric compares successive
available queries of the same scope; it is left undefined across an unavailable query.

## Preserved negative controls

All four incorrect policies fail in at least one scenario for every initial arrival
order. These counts describe a fixed test suite, not estimated failure probabilities.

| Candidate | Runs | Runs with violations | Violating event/query checks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Scoped candidate | 36 | 0 | 0 |
| Purge on loss of any grant | 36 | 12 | 72 |
| Ignore inference scope | 36 | 6 | 54 |
| Summary remains permanently usable/retained | 36 | 18 | 66 |
| Trust historical delivery grant | 36 | 30 | 102 |

The purge control destroys A despite a surviving alignment grant and independent
archive/audit rights. Alignment unnecessarily falls from (8,1/3) to (0,1), while other
contexts lose their data. This is a concrete loss caused by overbroad deletion.

Ignoring scope leaks B's alignment evidence into the archive/audit inference, and
later allows privately retained A to keep influencing alignment after its rights were
removed there. A valid grant somewhere is not a valid grant for the current operation.

The sticky-summary control keeps reporting (8,1/3) after P3, where the correct answer
is (0,1) or unavailable. It can therefore look numerically steady while concealing
forbidden information. The canonical JSONL files retain these exact events.

The historical-token control first fails the cutoff fixture by admitting a3 under
the old generation. Its estimate still equals (8,1/3), because current inference
rights exclude a3. The admission and retained-state checks detect the violation that
mean/variance checks miss. This distinction is especially relevant to a provider whose
responsibility includes storage and access, independently of the application's solver.

## Implications, limits and next action

This is evidence for a simple design boundary: generic scoped/versioned rights and
storage accounting in the provider, application-owned numerical identities, dependency
manifests, recomputation and explicit unavailable/partial results. Rights do not have
to become numerical factors, and solver covariance does not certify authorization.

The fixtures receive COMPLETE, trusted policy snapshots. They do not implement the
translation from a person's withdrawal intent into all affected raw and derivative
grants. In particular, P3 removes the mixed-summary grant explicitly; deleting a raw
grant alone is deliberately insufficient under P2. Binding immutable artifact versions
and their dependencies to that policy intent remains a contract/application obligation.
The provider must not infer this mathematical dependency by inspecting opaque bytes.
This is a limitation of the evidence, not a silently settled permission rule.

Identity/authentication and summary contents are trusted. Conflicting payloads are
detected while an identity is retained; authenticity after deletion is an external
premise. Retention cleanup means removal from this model's input store, not cryptographic
or process-memory erasure, and not erasure of already delivered results. Output-read/
publish roles, encrypted custody, transient computation without retention, offline
revocation, concurrent issuers, crashes, network delays and malicious participants
remain unimplemented. Newly applied policies are effective/learned/enforced in one
local event; an old replay is logged as learned and rejected, not enforced again.

For T01, the mixed-summary experiment confirms the rebuild-or-unavailable branch when
the policy forbids the old aggregate. It does not establish that every richer summary
must fail or extend the exact independent-scalar result to nonlinear elimination.
Partial overlap currently fails closed even when a raw rebuild might be possible.
No full R-family, recovery family, mandatory compound case or hardware claim is closed.

Next bounded cycle: I03 shared evidence and shared calibration. Compare source-ID
deduplication, a retained shared latent/joint representation, and authorized raw rebuild
against an independent exact batch answer. Include a diamond with duplicated evidence
and a case with disjoint observation IDs but a common uncertain calibration, so IDs
alone cannot be mistaken for statistical independence. Start with tiny linear examples;
leave feedback policy (I04), broader gauge tests (I05) and nonlinear models queued.
