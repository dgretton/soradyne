## Method

Simulate a scalar point mass in a fixed local frame with ideal independently authorized
position/velocity sensing. External alignment revises a task target in that frame.
Compare direct application, 0.5 s coalescing, 0.15 s persistence, a 0.05 m/s reference
transition, that transition with an explicit precision gate, and a deliberately
unauthorized retention control. Acceleration is bounded at 2 m/s^2. Integrate each
constant-acceleration segment analytically, including in-step stopping/maximum position.

Prescribed traces cover a 20 Hz revision storm over a 2 Hz external input frontier,
a necessary correction to an initially biased alignment, and P3 strict withdrawal.
The first two retain permission (P0); the third removes all alignment use rights.
The wall and true offset are evaluator-only inputs. The consumer does not see them.
No candidate is given simulator truth to select a method or decide when to brake.

The bound attached to a transitioned reference is latest_bound + |applied-latest|,
which exposes its lag under a triangle-inequality assumption. It is a deterministic
interval bound, not a probabilistic covariance. Numerical output availability and
permission are separate from the controller's physical ability to stop.

The protocol was written before the first simulation. No candidate parameter,
case or acceptance threshold was changed after seeing outcomes. Added two diagnostic
metrics after the initial run to exclude the common startup command from jerk/command
variation comparisons; these have no new acceptance threshold. Added a regression
test preserving the observed coalescing failures. A timestep validation ordering fix
ensures invalid/excessive step requests are rejected before simulation.

## Findings

### 1. Smoothness improved in the revision storm, but coalescing aliased the signal

There were 70 new revisions over 7 external input-frontier advances. No method treated
duplicate deliveries as new input; additional duplicate/stale replay reproduced every
trajectory exactly. Independent local controller sensing continued at 100 Hz.

| Method | Applied offset total variation (m) | Late target RMSE (m, 2-4 s) | Post-event peak commanded finite-difference jerk (m/s^3) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Direct | 16.680 | 0.008225 | 202.217 |
| Coalesce | 0.120 | 0.117940 | 105.327 |
| Persistence | 0 | 0.007801 | 9.327 |
| Slew | 0.175 | 0.007286 | 9.727 |

Persistence/slew met the predeclared >=75% variation reduction and <=0.05 m late
error criteria for this trace. Coalescing repeatedly sampled the same phase and kept
a biased mean: much less variation did not imply accurate task placement. Its error
bound exceeded the task budget during 1.75 s of continued following. All methods
stayed clear of the wall in this case, including direct: this case demonstrates
chatter, not a collision caused by the storm. Persistence benefits from the deliberately
correct initial estimate; it is not shown reliable against slowly biased evidence.

### 2. Delaying a necessary correction caused boundary crossings

At t=0.15 s, a new input corrected the offset from 0 to 0.4 m. The old input's claimed
0.02 m bound was intentionally wrong; every method had the same 0.15 s overconfident
lead-in. After that event, the reported bounds were conservative for this fixture.

| Method | Minimum clearance (m) | First boundary crossing (s) | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Direct | +0.162338 | none | Corrected target promptly; kept clear |
| Coalesce | -0.118644 | 0.740167 | Waited until t=0.5; response arrived too late |
| Persistence | +0.054846 | none | Delay reduced the available margin |
| Slew | -0.205079 | 0.731557 | Very smooth reference continued too far |
| Gated slew | +0.355000 | none | Exposed excess transition error at t=0.15 and braked |

Negative clearance means the simulated path entered the forbidden region; no collision
response is modeled after first entry, so deeper penetration is a diagnostic metric,
not a prediction of physical contact dynamics. Endpoint accuracy would miss coalescing's
failure: its late RMSE was only 0.009937 m, despite the earlier crossing.

Gated slew remained unavailable for the rest of the 4 s run and did not finish the
task (0.155 m short). It is a conservative fallback witness, not a selected solution.
It also produced a 400 m/s^3 finite-difference command jerk at the abrupt switch from
positive to maximum negative acceleration. Direct had 274 m/s^3 at the correction.
The simulated plant has no jerk limit, so the stopping result cannot be transferred
to an actuator with finite acceleration response. Lower reference jerk alone was
likewise insufficient: ungated slew had only 17.489 m/s^3 post-event commanded jerk,
yet crossed the boundary.

### 3. Strict withdrawal can be respected without reusing the reference for braking

At t=0.2 all admissible candidates cleared the alignment mean, bound and pending
transition and became unavailable at that same simulation step. Later estimate
messages did not restore permission. They used independent local velocity to brake,
stopped at t=0.526146, and retained 0.246410 m clearance. Their final position matched
the independent stopping-distance calculation x_event + v_event^2/(2 a_max).

The deliberately incorrect retention policy continued target following for 3.8 s.
It had good final task accuracy and no wall crossing, but the authorization metric
correctly rejected it. Accuracy and clearance alone would have selected an unlawful
policy under P3. The experiment does not claim that P3 is the only sensible product
policy; P1/P2 allow different retained-result behavior and remain to be investigated.

### 4. Refinement and harness checks

18 primary runs (3 cases x 6 modes), plus 36 runs at dt=0.005 and 0.0025 s. Every
crossing/no-crossing and unauthorized-use classification agreed across all timesteps.
For the necessary correction, coalescing clearance ranged from -0.118644 to -0.114342 m;
slew ranged from -0.205079 to -0.205270 m; direct from +0.162338 to +0.161944 m.
Gated stopping clearance remained +0.355 m. These are deterministic numerical
refinements, not 54 independent statistical trials or a coverage estimate.

23 total tests passed (9 earlier exact-information tests, 14 new dynamic/contract
tests). Independent checks cover constant acceleration, stopping distance, an interior
wall crossing missed by endpoint-only checking, inverse target transformation,
duplicate/stale replay and immediate invalidation. Bounds on simulation size are tested.

## Interpretation, tensions and limits

The evidence supports separating an authoritative estimate from a consumer transition,
making transition error visible, and delivering invalidation without a smoothing delay.
Fixed-rate coalescing needs more than a rate knob; it can retain bias or delay a newly
useful correction. This does not yet identify a production acceptance/replanning policy.
The provider transports versions and validity changes; it need not interpret geometry
or control mathematics.

T02 now has conditional dynamic evidence: prompt withdrawal plus local braking is
possible given independent authorized local state and adequate stopping capability.
It remains unresolved for finite jerk, reaction delay, insufficient clearance or
local state that itself depends on withdrawn data. No new architectural contradiction
is established. The model intentionally lacks actual sensing/inference, stochastic
covariance, obstacles/planning, actuator lag, SE(2)/SE(3), network faults and re-grants.
No claims about hardware safety, general closed-loop stability or collision probability
follow from these tests. Full S01/S03 and the matrix's compound cases remain open.
